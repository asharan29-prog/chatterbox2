# Coffee and Health: An Umbrella Review of Meta-Analyses

This umbrella review pooled 41 meta-analyses of coffee consumption and health outcomes in adults.

Moderate coffee consumption of three to four cups per day was associated with a lower risk of all-cause mortality compared with no coffee. Coffee drinkers also had a lower risk of type 2 diabetes in most of the included meta-analyses. Most of the underlying evidence is observational, so the review cannot prove that coffee causes these benefits. There is not enough evidence to conclude that coffee prevents heart disease.

Habitual coffee consumption is not associated with a lasting increase in blood pressure. Caffeine can raise blood pressure for a few hours in people who rarely consume it, but regular drinkers develop tolerance to this effect.

Unfiltered coffee, such as boiled or French press coffee, raises LDL cholesterol because it contains cafestol. Paper-filtered coffee removes most of the cafestol.

Pregnant people should limit caffeine to about 200 mg per day, because higher intake is associated with lower birth weight.

The overall certainty of the evidence was rated low to moderate.
