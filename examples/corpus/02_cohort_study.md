# The Lakeshore Cohort: Coffee Intake and Long-Term Outcomes

The Lakeshore Cohort followed 52,000 adults for eighteen years and recorded their coffee intake every four years.

Participants who drank three to four cups of coffee per day had a lower risk of death from any cause than non-drinkers. Regular coffee drinkers had a lower risk of type 2 diabetes, and the association was similar for decaffeinated coffee. This suggests that compounds other than caffeine, such as chlorogenic acids, may contribute.

Coffee intake was not associated with a higher risk of atrial fibrillation.

Heavy coffee intake above six cups per day was associated with slightly higher cholesterol in participants who drank unfiltered coffee.

Because this is an observational study, healthier lifestyles among coffee drinkers could explain part of the association.
